Academic portrait

A historian in the classroom and beyond.

Scholarship is recorded in publications and qualifications. A teacher’s deeper legacy is carried forward by students, ideas and intellectual habits.

Irfan Waheed Usmani’s academic work brings together teaching, historical research, scholarly writing and research supervision. His career at Government College University Lahore extends from Lecturer to Assistant Professor, accompanied by earlier experience in journalism and editorial work.

His teaching has covered an unusually broad historical range—from civilizations, Islamic history, medieval and colonial South Asia to political economy, historiography, research methodology, Pakistan’s political history, world history and international relations.

Alongside teaching, his scholarship has explored leftist movements in Pakistan, literary and political radicalism, Kashmir, colonialism, historiography, political history and the relationship between intellectual movements and the societies in which they emerge.

Across these roles runs a consistent academic concern: how historical knowledge is built, interpreted, written and passed forward.

Beyond titles

The full measure of academic work is larger than designation.

Academic life is often described through titles, positions and institutional roles. They matter, but they do not contain the whole measure of a scholar.

Some contributions are better understood through the students taught, questions opened, research guided, manuscripts improved, younger scholars encouraged, and intellectual standards quietly sustained over time.Recognition belongs to institutions and moments; intellectual influence belongs to generations.

Teaching as responsibility

Knowledge has its greatest value when it is shared.

For some academics, teaching is one responsibility among many. For others, it becomes the centre around which research, writing and mentorship acquire meaning. Dr. Usmani’s academic profile reflects sustained engagement with students across multiple levels—from introductory history to postgraduate research methodology and doctoral supervision.

The enduring value of such work appears in students who learn to question carefully, research honestly, write with discipline and carry knowledge forward.

The measure of an academic life

Some parts of a scholarly record do not fit inside a curriculum vitae.

A curriculum vitae records degrees, appointments, publications, awards and dates. It cannot fully record the conversations that changed a student’s direction, the thesis improved through patient guidance, or the habit of intellectual honesty passed quietly from teacher to student.

Those things do not always appear in institutional records. They are nevertheless part of the record that matters.

Titles describe a role. Character determines how that role is lived.
